Validating the Scatter Library Beyond Water Cylinders

The scatter library of notebook 10 (version 2) was built from water elliptical cylinders, with the scatter last sent by a Compton and by a Rayleigh scattering kept apart. Patients are not water: they are elliptical, off-centre, and made of lung, bone and soft tissue. The library stands every view of a patient in a water ellipse as thick and as wide as the patient's water there, takes each ray's kernel at the offset whose chord is the ray's water-equivalent length, and scales the kernel's Compton and Rayleigh parts by how much of each scattering the ray's own materials make into the angles the detector sees. Each kernel also gives up its own single scatter, computed in its water ellipse, for the ray's own, computed in the patient as its rays describe it: lungs and a spine move the scatter across the fan, and a water ellipse cannot. This notebook measures how well that holds: six phantoms that are not water cylinders, each simulated directly in Geant4 (every photon of the fan beam, every scattering scored by forced detection), against the library's prediction and against BasisSimulator v0.15.1's analytic model (which v0.16 replaced with the library), on a scanner with a 2-D grid (the SOMATOM Force) and one with a 1-D grid (the Aquilion ONE). The second thorax is held out: it was not used to choose anything. Every comparison is a number with a target; the notebook ends with its scorecard.

2. The Scans

The beam: BasisSimulator's IPEM spectrum at 120 kV through 9.2 mm of aluminium, 40 mm wide at the isocentre, no bowtie, one view (the beam along +y). The direct simulation fires the fan beam through each phantom placed at the scanner's isocentre, 48 million photons per case in 32 independent chunks (whose spread gives its own noise), and scores every scattering on the scanner's detector by forced detection (notebook 10), in whatever material it happened: water, lung, bone or a rod. The library's prediction takes rays every 0.1° across the fan, each with its water-equivalent length, its length through the outline and its materials' scattering and attenuation against water's; stands the view in its water ellipse; and sums the kernels, each at the offset whose chord is the ray's own water-equivalent length (a ray longer than the ellipse is thick gets an ellipse as thick as itself), each with its single scatter swapped for the ray's own in the patient as the rays describe it (notebook 10). BasisSimulator v0.15.1's analytic model, which v0.16 replaced with the library, is run on the same projection, one ray per column of the detector, with its size scaling at the phantom's water-equivalent diameter.

exact_primary (generic function with 1 method)
"""The exact primary per photon in each column of the central row: Beer–Lambert through every material."""
function exact_primary(case, s, row_share)
    p = at_isocenter(case.phantom.phantom, s)
    e, w = spectrum
    weights = w ./ sum(w)
    cols = MC.column_edges(s)
    centres = (cols[1:(end - 1)] .+ cols[2:end]) ./ 2
    μ(m) = [MC.linear_attenuation(m, Float64(E)) for E in 1:150]
    cache = Dict{String, Vector{Float64}}()
    function column_primary(γ)
        abs(γ) <= s.fan_deg / 2 || return 0.0
        lengths = MC.material_lengths(p, (0.0, 0.0), (sind(γ), cosd(γ)))
        detected = sum(
            wk * exp(-sum(get!(() -> μ(m), cache, m.name)[clamp(round(Int, Ek), 1, 150)] * L for (m, L) in lengths)) *
                s.efficiency[clamp(round(Int, Ek), 1, 150)] * Ek
                for (Ek, wk) in zip(e, weights)
        )
        return s.column_bin_deg / s.fan_deg * row_share * detected
    end
    return [column_primary(γ) for γ in centres]
end
