# Changelog

All notable changes to BasisSimulator.jl will be documented in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/).
Entries from v0.1.2 to v0.14.0 were generated by
[release-please](https://github.com/googleapis/release-please) from
[Conventional Commits](https://www.conventionalcommits.org/); from v0.15.0
onward they are written by hand, and releases are cut by hand — see
[RELEASING.md](RELEASING.md). Every pull request adds its user-visible changes under
`[Unreleased]`.

## [Unreleased]

### Breaking
- **The energy-integrating detector integrates energy.** Each photon of energy `E` leaves a mean signal
  `m₁ = E·efficiency` and a mean square `m₂ = m₁²/dqe0` (`eid_signal_moments`). One projector walk forms both
  spectral sums of every ray, `S₁ = Σ w̄ m₁ B e^{−L}` and `S₂ = Σ w̄ m₂ B e^{−L}`; the detector reads
  `λ = I₀·ff·S₁` with Gaussian noise of variance `I₀·ff·S₂ + σ_electronic²`. Before, it counted photons
  (`Σ w̄ η B e^{−L}`, Poisson variance = mean) and applied η twice (in the sum and again in `I₀`). In air the noise
  σ falls by 2.7 % (SOMATOM Force, 120 kV: η̄² = 0.788 → DQE(0) = 0.832), 2.2 % (Force, 80 kV), 7.0 % (Definition
  Flash) and 2.8 % (Revolution Apex); behind water it now rises with the dispersion `S₂/S₁` (Force, 120 kV: 1.203 in
  air, 1.429 behind 20 cm and 1.501 behind 30 cm, all relative to the air beam's mean signal), so behind 30 cm σ is
  ≈ 9 % above v0.15.1. Mean CT numbers of non-water materials change through the energy weighting (`m₁ ∝ E`).
- **The Geant4 detector tables replace the MCNP ones**: `GEMSTONE_MC_EFFICIENCY_LUT`, `UFC_MC_EFFICIENCY_LUT` and
  `UFC_FLASH_MC_EFFICIENCY_LUT` now hold BasisMonteCarlo's flat-field Geant4 runs (`energies`, `efficiency`,
  `dqe0`, `thickness_mm`), copied in as plain data: Gemstone at 6.40 g/cm³ and 3.0 mm, UFC at 7.31 g/cm³ and
  1.4 mm for both Siemens scanners. `SOMATOM_DEFINITION_FLASH.detector_depth` is 1.4 mm (was 1.0). A table is
  made at one depth, so `DetectorEfficiency(material, thickness_mm, MC_LUT)` refuses another;
  `detector_efficiency_mode = :beer_lambert` models any depth (`m₁ = E·q`, `m₂ = E²·q`). The Beer–Lambert
  attenuation tables are scaled to the same densities.
- **Removed:** `compute_eid_efficiency_vector`, `get_gemstone_mc_efficiency`, `get_ufc_mc_efficiency`,
  `get_ufc_flash_mc_efficiency` (the tables are the exported `*_MC_EFFICIENCY_LUT`s; the response is internal); `detector_efficiency_gemstone`, `detector_efficiency_ufc`,
  `detector_efficiency_ufc_flash` (→ `DetectorEfficiency(material, thickness_mm, mode)` with the scanner's own
  `detector_material` and `detector_depth`; its `fill_factor` field is gone, the scanner's is the one);
  `apply_fill_factor!` (the fill factor scales the quanta once, in the noise); `get_scintillator_mu`'s silent
  Gemstone fallback for an unknown material (it throws) and its aliases (`"lumex"`, `"GOS"`, `"UFC_Flash"`, …).
- **`EICTWorkspace` fields:** `η_vec`, `wη_gpu`, `η_eff`, `σ_e_photon` and `air_scan` are gone; `m1`, `m2` (keV,
  keV²), `wm1_gpu`, `wm2_gpu`, `dispersion` (the per-ray `S₂/S₁` of the last projection) and `σ_electronic`
  (electronic noise ÷ gain, in units of the air beam's mean signal) are new; `bowtie_air_reference` is
  `air_reference` and always built. `keep_projection` keeps the dispersion too.

- **The photon-counting detector is the NAEOTOM Alpha's, read at its native dexels.** Its response is BasisMonteCarlo's
  Geant4 native-dexel table (`PCCT_NATIVE_TABLE_PATH`, plain data: transported charge, 1.9 keV electronic noise, the
  2-D anti-scatter grid of 4 × 6-dexel cells, open fraction 0.716), the cell's mean dexel per incident photon. Each
  native dexel's position in its cell sets its gain (`pcct_position_gain`, folded into the per-ray source
  transmission, so air still reads zero). v0.15.1's `cdte_response_v4` is test data only (`test/data/`).
- **Counting noise per native dexel, then a digital 2 × 2 sum** (`pcct_count!`, `pcct_event_classes`): each dexel's
  unshared counts and, per pair of neighbours and pair of bins, the photons they share, all Poisson. At 120 kV a dexel
  shares 5.6 % of its counts with each neighbour across the fan, 5.3 % along z and 0.7 % diagonally (24 % in all).
  Measured in air: a UHR dexel's Fano factor 1.004 and its neighbour correlation 0.052 (as predicted); a standard
  pixel's Fano factor above 20 keV 1.109, within 3 % of the binned table's; standard counts = 4.000 × UHR counts.
- **Pile-up per native dexel, before the sum**, re-implemented from the fitted inputs (pulses per photon and their
  heights, a 10 ns CR-RC shaper, one retriggering comparator per threshold, 27 ns dead time):
  `compute_mc_pileup_matrix(thresholds, weights, energies, rate, dead_time_ns; n_pulses, seed)`, applied by
  `pcct_count!`. Count loss at 140 kV: 2.6 % at 1 Mcps per dexel, 21 % at 10 Mcps.
- **η once, no cap:** the response is per incident photon (absorption included), so `quantum_efficiency` no longer
  multiplies the projection, the air response, the scatter weights or `compute_mc_count_moments` (its `η` keyword is
  gone; `compute_scatter_bin_weights(energies, weights, energy_weights, R, kVp)`); `compute_mc_drm` no longer
  renormalises rows above one (a shared photon counts in two dexels).
- **`NAEOTOM_ALPHA` as documented:** pitch 0.373 × 0.399 mm at the isocentre (was 0.301 × 0.353, the electrode
  without the grid's gaps), 1376 columns (was 1659), 144 rows × 0.4 mm = 57.6 mm, dead time 27 ns (was 5).
  **`NAEOTOM_ALPHA_UHR`** (new): native dexels, 120 rows of 0.2 mm, 2752 columns, the 20 keV threshold.
- **`PCCTScanner` fields removed:** `fill_factor_row`, `fill_factor_col` (unused; the dexel's aperture and the grid
  are the table's), `n_energy_bins` (it is `length(energy_thresholds)`), `energy_resolution`, `charge_sharing_fwhm`
  (the table's), `pixel_mode` (dead), `native_dexel_col_mm`, `native_dexel_row_mm` (a native dexel's pitch is the
  pixel's over `binning_factor`; its aperture is the table's). Thresholds must be among the table's 16.
- **Removed:** `apply_pcct_noise!` (→ `pcct_count!` inside `simulate!`) and `simulate!`'s `noise_rng` (every
  draw has its own counter-based stream), `apply_pcct_pileup!` (→ `pcct_count!`'s pile-up per native dexel),
  `simulate_pulse_train`, `PileupResult`; `MCResponseData`'s unused `R_perpixel`, `Var_total` and `Cov_pixpair`.

- **Pile-up is corrected outside `simulate!`, from simulated calibration scans:** `calibrate_pileup` (air scans at
  1/16 to 2 × the protocol's current, the pile-up-free counts extrapolated linearly from the lowest) →
  `apply_pileup!`. `PCCTScanner`'s `pileup_correction`, `apply_pcct_pileup_correction!` and the result's `pileup_S`
  are removed (the correction no longer reads the model's own matrices). On a 20 cm water cylinder at a clinical
  rate the rms log error against a pile-up-free scan falls from 0.096 to 0.016.
- **`SimOptions(detector_efficiency_mode)`** is `:mc_lut` (default) or `:beer_lambert`; `:auto` (the same as
  `:mc_lut`) is gone and anything else throws.
- **`fill_factor_standard` is removed**, with the fallback it served: an `EICTScanner`'s fill factors must lie in
  (0, 1] (zero meant "unset").

- **One anode angle, the scanner's.** `CTProtocol`'s `anode_angle` field and keyword are removed: the anode is
  hardware, `scanner.target_angle`, which now also picks the spectrum (the bundled IPEM table at the nearest
  tabulated angle, 8° or 10°). `resolve_source_spectrum_without_bowtie` requires `scanner`. `NAEOTOM_ALPHA`'s
  anode is 8° (documented; was 7° geometry with a 10° spectrum); the SOMATOM Definition Flash (7°) and the
  default `EICTScanner`/`PCCTScanner` (7°) now read the 8° table where a protocol defaulted to 10°.
- **`PhotonCountingDetector`** keeps only what is read: `material`, `thickness_mm`, `pixel_size_mm`,
  `energy_thresholds_keV`, `dead_time_ns`, `binning_factor` (gone: `energy_resolution_keV`,
  `charge_sharing_fwhm_mm`, `enable_charge_sharing`, `enable_pile_up`, `enable_anti_coincidence`,
  `coincidence_window_ns`, `electronic_noise_keV`, `seed`, all informational).
- **Public names against v0.15.1:** added `calibrate_flat_field`, `apply_flat_field!`, `calibrate_pileup`,
  `apply_pileup!`, `NAEOTOM_ALPHA_UHR` (and a `spectral_basis` method); removed `PileupResult`, `apply_fill_factor!`,
  `apply_pcct_noise!`, `apply_pcct_pileup!`, `apply_pcct_pileup_correction!`, `compute_eid_efficiency_vector`,
  `detector_efficiency_gemstone`, `detector_efficiency_ufc`, `detector_efficiency_ufc_flash`,
  `fill_factor_standard`, `get_gemstone_mc_efficiency`, `get_ufc_flash_mc_efficiency`, `get_ufc_mc_efficiency`,
  `simulate_pulse_train`. The new plumbing (`eid_signal_moments`, the native table, `pcct_count!`,
  `pcct_event_classes`, `pcct_position_gain`) is internal.

- **Patient scatter is the Geant4 scatter library**, superposed on each acquisition's own rays; the analytic
  convolution model (Ohnesorge-style kernel, `(diameter/30)^1.5` and air-gap scalings) is gone. BasisMonteCarlo's v2
  library (notebook 10: pencil beams in water elliptical cylinders, Compton, wide- and forward-Rayleigh parts kept
  apart, path lengths by energy, per-column moments) is read as plain data (TOML header + little-endian Float32,
  BasisSimulator's own copy of the reader, `read_scatter_library`) and superposed as its `library_scan` does: each
  view in the water ellipse of its rays (`view = :mean`, the geometric mean of the water-equivalent and the outline
  ellipses), each ray at the impact whose chord is its water-equivalent length, its Compton and Rayleigh parts scaled
  by its materials against water (the library's element table and the scanner's angle profile) and by how much less
  gets out of them, moved to its own column. The rays are the phantom's own, walked per material
  (`dd_fast_material_paths!`) on coarse rays: 0.25° across the fan, groups of three library rows along z, views every
  10°; each dexel interpolates between the library's 2° × 10–20 mm bins and the coarse views. An energy integrator's
  scatter enters `S₁` (its `m₁`-weighted keV over `E_ref`) and `S₂` (`m₂/m₁` at the photon's energy), so its noise
  carries the scatter; a photon counter's enters each bin's expected counts before the noise and pile-up, a photon
  counted by the library split over the bins as the response table splits a photon of its energy (`R(E, b)/η(E)`).
  Against BasisMonteCarlo's own `library_scan` on the same rays and data (SOMATOM Force, Aquilion ONE; 32 cm cylinder,
  36 × 26 cm ellipse) the superposition agrees to 1e-6 per bin; against `library_scan` on exact rays every 0.05° to
  ≤ 1.7 % per bin (4 % at the patient-edge peak of a 20 cm cylinder on the Aquilion), 0.9 % at the centre.
- **The scatter correction estimates from the data alone.** The energy integrator's DAS and the photon counter
  (`scatter_correction = true` on either) measure every coarse ray's water-equivalent length from its measured
  transmission (scatter included, inverted through the ray's spectrum), superpose the scatter of that water patient
  (`view = :wet`), subtract it and measure again (two passes). Before, the energy integrator subtracted exactly the
  scatter it had injected and the photon counter re-ran the analytic model; now the correction is realistic, not
  exact (residual on a 30 cm water cylinder: a few % of the scatter).
- **Each scanner names its scatter kernels**: `EICTScanner(; scatter_kernels = "")` and
  `PCCTScanner(; scatter_kernels = "")`, the library entry (`"SOMATOM Force"`, `"SOMATOM Definition Flash"`,
  `"NAEOTOM Alpha"`, `"Revolution Apex"`, `"Brilliance iCT"`, `"Aquilion ONE"`). `SOMATOM_FORCE`, `SOMATOM_DEFINITION_FLASH`,
  `REVOLUTION_APEX` and `NAEOTOM_ALPHA` set theirs, and their `_B`/`_UHR` copies inherit it; `use_scatter = true` on a
  scanner with `""` throws, naming the scanners that have kernels. `scatter_kernels(entry; path)` loads an entry
  (cached per process) from its artifact, one per scanner (about 250 MB, Julia artifact store, SHA-256 verified,
  `SCATTER_KERNEL_ARTIFACTS`), or from a local library with `path`. Outside the library's grid (thickness
  140–500 mm, aspect 0.6–2.5, 20–140 keV) the nearest kernels stand in, with a one-time warning; an ellipse thinner
  than 140 mm takes the thinnest kernel scaled by the water its ray crosses.
- **Scatter is on by default: `SimOptions(; use_scatter = true)`**, as on a real scanner. The clinical constants name
  their kernels; a scanner built from scratch names none (`scatter_kernels = ""`), so simulating it now throws an
  `ArgumentError` unless its options say `use_scatter = false` (or it starts from a constant, `EICTScanner(SOMATOM_FORCE;
  …)`). Calibration scans (`calibrate_flat_field`, `calibrate_pileup`) still run without scatter. Until the kernels'
  release, load a local copy of the library with `scatter_kernels(entry; path = "…/<entry>.toml")` before the first
  scan. `SimOptions(o; kwargs...)` is the copy constructor.
- **`EICTScanner(; scatter_correction = true)`**, the same field, meaning and default as `PCCTScanner`'s: the DAS
  subtracts the scatter it estimates from the measured signal, and `false` leaves the scatter in the data (before, an
  energy integrator always corrected). **`PCCTScanner`'s `scatter_correction` defaults to `true`** (was `false`).
  `EICTWorkspace` has a `scatter_correction` field. `PCCTScanner` rejects negative `gain_spread` and
  `threshold_spread_keV`, as `EICTScanner` does its spreads.
- **Docs:** notebooks 10 (titanium implant) and 11 (helical), whose generic 540/950 mm scanners have no scatter
  kernels, scan variants of `REVOLUTION_APEX` at its 625.6/1100 mm, keeping their detectors' rows and columns; the
  getting-started photon-counting scanner is `PCCTScanner(NAEOTOM_ALPHA; …)`, its narrowed, unbinned, ideal detector
  kept. The notebooks drop `use_scatter = true`; notebook 06 (CatSim) keeps `use_scatter = false`.
- **Removed:** `ScatterModel`, `geometry_aware_scatter_model`, `create_scatter_kernel_spatial`,
  `estimate_phantom_diameter_cm`, `estimate_scatter_field!`, `compute_scatter_energy_weights`, `inject_scatter!`,
  `inject_scatter_bins!`, `compute_scatter_bin_weights` and the `SCATTER_REF_*` constants; the `phantom` keyword of
  `build_physics_config` and `resolve_source_spectrum_full`; `PhysicsConfig.scatter` holds `ScatterKernels`;
  `EICTWorkspace`'s `scatter_kernel`, `scatter_kernel_1d`, `scatter_temp` and `PCCTWorkspace`'s `combined` are gone,
  `scatter` (a `ScatterPlan`, or `nothing`) is new on both; `keep_projection` keeps no scatter field (the scatter is
  in the projection). A phantom simulated with scatter has at most 64 materials (`compact_materials`).
- **The photon-counting detection runs on the scan's device, in its precision (Float32).** Every native dexel's
  expected counts, its defects, its counting noise (each pair class's shared photons), its pile-up and the digital
  2 × 2 sum are kernels on the phantom's backend (`pcct_count!` does the noise, the pile-up and the sum in one; the
  CPU backend runs the same kernels); before, they ran on the host in Float64, about 7.3 GB of host memory per bin
  for a full NAEOTOM Alpha scan. Each Poisson draw has its own counter-based stream keyed by (seed, class, dexel,
  view), so a seed fixes a scan on a backend whatever the thread count, and the CPU and a GPU draw nearly the same
  realisation; the realisations differ from before, their statistics do not. Against the host code on a 160 × 8-pixel
  Alpha (300 views, defects and pile-up on): the noise-free counts agree to 5e-7, the noisy means, Fano factors and
  neighbour correlations within sampling error, and the flat-field and pile-up residuals of `test/defects.jl` to
  6 digits. On notebook 04's detector (1376 × 17 pixels, 120 views × 5 sub-views) the detection takes 0.07 s (was
  8.8 s) and no host memory (was 0.7 GB). `pileup_counts!` (internal) is folded into `pcct_count!`
  (`pcct_count!(counts, expected, classes; seed, binning, pileup)`, `classes = nothing` for the expected counts);
  `PCCTWorkspace`'s unused `noise_staging` and `rng` are gone and its `defects` live on the device.
- **The calibrations scan on the scan's device:** `calibrate_flat_field` and `calibrate_pileup` take
  `like`, an array on the device to scan on (the scan's `phantom.mask`; the CPU by default), and
  `apply_pileup!` is a kernel on the bins' backend. On notebook 04's detector `calibrate_pileup` (40 views) takes
  13.5 s (was 342 s) and `calibrate_flat_field` (120 views) 1.5 s (was 145 s); the gains and curves agree with the
  host code to 6e-7. Notebook 04's whole acquisition (both calibrations and three draws, 1200 views × 5 sub-views,
  scatter on) takes 505 s (was 3462 s) and 3.8 GB of host memory (was 12.6 GB); a full NAEOTOM Alpha scan
  (1376 × 144 pixels, 1152 views) fits in 6.7 GB of host and 44.5 GB of device memory, its detection about 2 s.

### Fixed
- **The air calibration without a bowtie** read `−log η̄` (≈ 0.12 for the Force) in every ray; it reads zero.
- **The fill factor** was in the counts but not the air reference, and taken back out by a post-log offset; it is
  applied once, to the quanta (`I₀·ff`).
- **The electronic noise** was converted to photons with the spectrum's mean energy; it is in keV (÷ gain), in the
  signal's own units.
- **An empty field scattered** (the EICT scatter was estimated from a sinogram carrying the bowtie and the detector
  response); the library's superposition leaves gases out of the patient, so air scatters nothing.
- **A large photon-counting scan threw a `BoundsError`** in the fused spectral projectors (`:dd_fast`, `:dd`,
  Siddon): bin b sits at offset (b − 1) × rays in one flat buffer, and that Int32 offset wrapped once it passed
  2³¹, as the NAEOTOM Alpha's full detector does at native resolution from 904 views on. The offset is an `Int`.

### Added
- **Dexel defects and their flat field.** `EICTScanner(; gain_spread, offset_spread_keV, defect_seed)` and
  `PCCTScanner(; gain_spread, threshold_spread_keV, defect_seed)`: each dexel's own gain and offset (energy
  integrator) or gain and threshold offsets (photon counter, per native dexel, its counts read off the table at
  thresholds `(t + δ)/g`, weighted between the air beam and one behind 20 cm of water by the ray's own line
  integral). The struct defaults are zero; `NAEOTOM_ALPHA` (and `NAEOTOM_ALPHA_UHR`) is a realistic unit, with
  `gain_spread = 0.02` and `threshold_spread_keV = 0.35` (BasisMonteCarlo notebook 03), and its docs notebooks
  calibrate it; an ideal detector is a copy with the spreads at zero. Corrected by
  `calibrate_flat_field` (simulated air scans: two currents for an energy integrator, which separates offset from
  gain; one low-flux scan for a photon counter) → `apply_flat_field!`. Residuals against the ideal detector on a
  20 cm water cylinder: energy integrator (2 % gain, offset σ 2 × 10⁴ keV per reading) < 1e-4 rms, exact as gain and offset are
  energy-blind; photon counter (2 % gain, 0.35 keV thresholds) 0.0287 → 0.0073 rms log, the rest the offsets'
  different weight on a hardened beam.

- `spectral_basis(ws::EICTWorkspace, protocol)`: the
  one-channel basis of an energy-integrating acquisition, its air response scaled to the noise-equivalent quanta
  `I₀·ff·S₁²/S₂`; `spectral_basis_from_acquisitions` takes these directly for dual-kVp and dual-source pairs.
- Tests (`test/eict_signal.jl`, CPU): the air scan's variance against `S₂/(I₀·ff·S₁²)` with and without a bowtie
  (ratio 1 ± 0.02); NEQ in air = ff·N·DQE(0) to 2 % (Force, 120 kV: DQE(0) = 0.832); the variance behind 20 and
  30 cm of water against `D/λ` and the rising dispersion; the electronic term; water BHC against a 70 keV scan
  (< 1 HU, cupping < 2 HU); and a noise-free dual-kVp decomposition returning the water path.
- Tests (`test/scatter.jl`, on a synthetic library written in the library's format by `test/scatter_library.jl`): the
  reader's round trip, the kernels' geometry and clamped interpolation, the error for a scanner with no kernels, a
  centred cylinder's symmetric profile, linearity in mAs, both families' units, the data-driven correction, an
  empty field (no scatter), and CPU/GPU agreement.

### Changed
- **AcceleratedKernels 0.5 is allowed alongside 0.4:** `AcceleratedKernels = "0.4, 0.5"`, so BasisSimulator installs in

## [0.15.0](https://github.com/MolloiLab/BasisSimulator.jl/compare/v0.14.0...v0.15.0) (2026-09-24)

Everything since 0.14.0, the last registered release. The work landed on `main` in stages whose
headings below carry the version numbers `Project.toml` held at the time (0.15.0 to 0.18.0);
none of those was registered or tagged, so they ship together as 0.15.0, the next version the
General registry accepts after 0.14.0. The stages are newest first. Each stage's Breaking items are
breaking relative to the stage before it; those on API added since 0.14.0 (the VMI chain,
`vmi_pipeline`) do not affect a 0.14.0 user.

### The release pull request: clean-up, documentation, release process

### After stage 0.18.0: the spectral denoising chain and view integration

The chain of basis-spectral-denoising (its final version), in the package.

#### Added
- **`SpectralHYPR`, generalized HYPR-LR in both domains, for `vmi_pipeline(; denoiser)`.**
  `ProjectionHYPR(; kernel = HYPRKernel((3, 3)), dispersion = :measured, view_stride = 2)` pools
  each ray's split of its total count over its (column × view) neighbours within a detector row,
  with total-count likelihood weights and the dispersion measured from the air rays
  (`estimate_dispersion`); `view_stride = 2` pools views of one parity, so the odd/even halves the
  image-domain noise estimates rely on stay independent. `ImageHYPR(; candidates, noise_window)`
  keeps the composite (the minimum-noise VMI) as reconstructed and pools its noise-independent
  complement in plane at a local noise map, over the window among `candidates` with the least
  estimated full-data risk (an unbiased half-view estimate). Window profiles are specified like FBP
  apodization: `BoxProfile`, `TriangleProfile`, `CustomProfile` in `HYPRKernel`. Also exported:
  `hypr_lr`, `image_hypr`, `guided_pool`, `local_noise`.
- **`PairFilter(composite, complement)`, `spectral_pair`, `acnr_complement!`.** The FDK windows go on
  the composite and its complement, whose noise is uncorrelated (a window per water / iodine image
  would stop their anti-correlated noise cancelling). `spectral_pair` reconstructs the pair and its
  odd/even halves; ACNR acts on the complement only (the composite restored), before the
  image-domain instance. `vmi_pipeline(; fbp_filter = PairFilter(…), pair_basis, composite_energy)`
  fixes the pair and the composite's energy for a scanner and protocol; the order is FDK → ACNR
  (complement) → image HYPR. basis-vmi's single-window chain is unchanged.
- **View integration, for every detector family.** `SimOptions(; view_samples, view_arc)`: each view
  reads the intensity averaged over the arc the gantry turns while it integrates (`view_samples`
  sub-views, the midpoint rule; `view_arc` the fraction of the view spacing — the duty cycle of one
  energy for rapid kVp switching), a blur of the signal the noise does not share. Default 1 = point
  views, unchanged. `rotate_geometry`, `view_sample_offsets`.
- **A projection shared by noise draws:** `simulate!(…; keep_projection = true)` returns everything
  before the noise (`projection`), and `simulate!(…; projection)` re-uses it — skipping the forward
  projection, view integration, focal spot and scatter — bit-identically to simulating again with
  that call's seed, on both detector families.
- `create_workspace(::EICTScanner, …)` forwards to `create_eict_workspace`, so every scanner is built
  the same way.

#### Changed
- `vmi_pipeline`'s `use_acnr` defaults to `true` (with or without a denoiser).
- `material_paths` caches point views only; passing a cache with `view_samples > 1` is an error that

- `estimate_dispersion` takes the air rays as those below 5 % attenuation, each estimating the

### Stage 0.18.0 (branch fix/scanner-fidelity, #72)

Scanner fidelity, first part. Every simulation's numbers change; see each item.

#### Changed
- **The quarter-detector offset is honoured.** `Scanner.detector_col_offset` (columns) was stored
  and never read: every projector, the fan weighting, every backprojector and the bowtie assumed
  a centred detector. `CTGeometry` now carries `column_offset` (18th field; positional
  constructors default it to 0), `column_center(T, geom)` is read everywhere, and every geometry
  rebuild (subsets, HIR support, FOV override, PCCT native grid — rescaled by the binning factor —
  the VMI single-row geometry) carries it. With the default of 0.25 the rays of every simulation
  move a quarter column and opposing views interleave. The field is in COLUMNS: the value
  `detector_col_offset = pixel_col_iso / 2` (millimetres) in the notebooks and a test was wrong
  and is now 0.25.
- **HIR's internal support covers the scan circle.** The PWLS work grid extends in plane to the
  circle the fan sees (`scan_circle_diameter`), at the requested pixel size, with the cone halo
  from that radius; the requested grid is a window of it (`output_x/y/z`). A body wider than the
  requested circle no longer piles its attenuation onto the circle's edge (+155 HU over FBP on a
  32 × 14 cm body at a 20 cm request; +11 HU EICT / +120 HU PCCT-VMI at a chest wall at 35 cm),
  and HIR's answer no longer depends on the requested FOV (the same body read +0.1 / −7.4 /
  −7.8 HU in its interior at 20 / 30 / 39 cm; FBP −2.6 at all three). A warm start
  (`init_volume`) seeds the rest of the support by FDK. Cost: on a 512² @ 35 cm request the work
  grid is ~714² × (1.3× the planes): HIR takes ~2.5× longer and its four work arrays ~2.5× the
  memory (0.7 GiB for the NAEOTOM protocol).
- **Photon-counting bowtie across the fan; `I0` per ray.** The PCCT path folded the bowtie in at
  the fan centre only, so every ray got the central flux (air-scan edge / centre counts 1.00
  where the bowtie transmits 0.10). The fused spectral kernel now receives the per-ray source
  transmission (as the EICT path does), and the air response is per ray and bin:
  `I0[col, row, b]`. `simulate!`'s `I0_bins` is that `[n_cols, n_rows, n_bins]` array;
  `apply_pcct_noise!`, `inject_scatter_bins!` (which takes the per-ray whole-spectrum air
  response `I0_all`), `combine_pcct_bin_counts!` (returns per-group `[n_cols, n_rows]` matrices; removed in the release
  pull request, above),
  `spectral_basis(ws)` (ray-resolved through `ws.bowtie_spectral`; the keyword is `I0`) and
  `spectral_basis_from_bins` (`I0`, `transmission`) take it. Scatter counts scale with the ray's
  own flux. Air reads 0 in every column and bin.
- **`calibrate_pcct_poly_bhc` fits each column on the spectrum that column saw** (`W·bt[col, mid-row, e]`),
  as the energy-integrating calibration does: with the bowtie per ray the summed-bin image of a
  35 cm water body read +9 HU at the centre and −51 HU at the rim under the former single
  spectrum; a water cylinder now reads water across the fan (test).
- **Pile-up at each ray's own count rate.** One MC migration matrix at the central air rate was
  applied to every ray, including rays through 35 cm of patient. `pileup_S` is now
  `[n_bins, n_bins, n_rates]` on a grid LINEAR in rate — `S(0) = I` exactly and Monte-Carlo
  matrices at ¼, ½, ¾ and 1 of the brightest ray's incident rate (`pileup_rates`,
  `pileup_rate_air`; the measured loss is linear in rate·τ, 0.092 at 0.10); `apply_pcct_pileup!`
  blends each ray's matrix from its truth counts relative to the brightest ray's air counts and
  `apply_pcct_pileup_correction!(bins, I0, S, rates, rate_air)` inverts that same matrix (three
  fixed-point rounds; residual < 5e-4 at rate·τ = 0.1).
- **Binned detectors (`binning_factor > 1`): counts are physical.** The kernel's spectral matrix
  is per native dexel (`W / bf²`) so the binned sum is the photons a binned pixel receives; the
  noise, pile-up and scatter therefore see physical counts. (On the branch's first version the
  per-ray `I0` was `bf²` × physical, which would have drawn the noise at 4× the photons.)
- **The heel effect runs along the cone (rows), not the fan.** `heel_effect.jl` mapped the
  takeoff angle to the fan angle of the column (5× across a 48 cm fan); the anode axis of a
  third-generation CT is z, so the gradient is along the rows, flat along the fan, anode on the
  `+z` side. The mean production depth is 1 µm (2 %/degree of cone angle, the textbook order;
  the former 10 µm gave 20 %/degree and was hidden by the fan mapping's angle clamp), and
  `simulate!`'s heel now uses it (it built a 10 µm model directly). An anode angle that does not
  exceed the half-cone (a 7° anode on a 160 mm cone) is refused as an invalid tube geometry.
#### Added
- `column_offset`, `column_center`, `scan_circle_diameter`, `apply_pcct_pileup!`.
- Tests: `detector_offset.jl` (offset physics through dd, ddᵀ, the row-tiled path and Siddon; labels
  through the affine stay on the image to 0.1 px), `hir_support.jl`, `pcct_bowtie.jl` (incl. a
  binned detector), `heel_axis.jl`.
#### Known, documented, not yet changed on this branch
- Helical HIR keeps the requested circle as support (axial single-slice requests are extended).
- Scatter behind a bowtie is scaled by the receiving ray's flux (a modelling choice shared with
  the energy-integrating path): the scatter reaching a low-flux edge pixel from the high-flux
  centre is under-estimated there.
- `estimate_pcct_workspace_bytes` does not yet count the per-ray transmission tables and `I0`.
- HIR's data step at strength 60 is small on the wide grid (≈0.6 % of the residual per
  sub-iteration): "HIR 60" is close to FDK plus Huber smoothing; the strength table's "PWLS"

### Stage 0.17.0 (2026-09-18)

#### Breaking

* **The FBP kernel is bandlimited to the reconstruction grid.** Every apodization window —
  CatSim standard/soft/bone/custom and the classical Shepp-Logan, cosine, Hamming, Hann — was
  defined against the detector's Nyquist frequency `1/(2Δ_det)`. On a detector finer than the
  image grid that passes frequencies the grid cannot represent, and the backprojector folds them
  back into the image as fine grain: a NAEOTOM-like photon-counting detector samples 0.30 mm at
  isocentre against a 0.68 mm grid (512² over 35 cm), 2.3× the grid's Nyquist, and its FBP images
  were visibly grainier than the physical scanner's at the same σ. Now `grid_bandlimit(geom,
  volume_size) = min(1, Δ_ray / Δ_grid)` is computed from the geometry by `fdk_reconstruct`, the
  helical WFBP chain (against the rebinned parallel spacing) and both reconstruction workspaces
  (so HIR's initialiser too), and `create_spatial_kernel(…; bandlimit)` stretches the window over
  `[0, bandlimit]` of the sampling Nyquist and zeroes the response above it. A named kernel
  therefore means the same resolution on any detector. **Reconstructions on a detector finer than
  the grid change** (less noise, no aliased grain; CT numbers unchanged — tested on a water disk);
  on a detector no finer than the grid the CatSim kernels are bit-identical and the ramp identical
  to roundoff. `filter_sinogram!`/`filter_sinogram` take `bandlimit` (default 1, the old
  behaviour) for callers that build their own chain.
* **The classical windows are the actual filters now.** Shepp-Logan, cosine, Hamming and Hann were
  spatial-domain approximations (`sinc(k/n)` and cosines of `k/n` multiplying the taps — the
  Shepp-Logan one was not the Shepp-Logan filter). All windows go through one frequency-domain
  path, `frequency_window(filter, f)` on `[0, 1]` — `sinc(f/2)`, `cos(πf/2)`,
  `0.54 + 0.46 cos(πf)`, `½(1 + cos(πf))` — so those four filters produce different (correct)
  kernels. `apply_spatial_window!` and `_apply_catsim_freq_window!` are gone.

#### Added

* `grid_bandlimit`, `frequency_window`, `apply_frequency_window!`; `test/filtering.jl`.

Everything 0.15.0 claimed was re-derived independently — the dose Monte Carlo against IEC

#### Breaking

* **The VMI chain is no longer limited to one detector row and one slice.** Nothing in it ever had
  to be: T-LBF's neighbourhood is (column, view) and never crosses rows, ACNR works slice by slice,
  and the synthesis is per voxel. The limits came from porting the basis-vmi use case — a
  z-invariant phantom whose rows are summed into one slice — and are gone:
  `tlbf_denoise` filters each detector row in its own plane (bit-identical to the single-row filter
  on each, tested), `vmi_pipeline` keeps every row unless `reduce_rows = true` and reconstructs onto
  whatever grid it is given, and `synthesize_vmi_stack` returns `(nx, ny, nz, n_energies)`.
  Consequences for callers: `vmis` is four-dimensional, so a single slice's energy `e` is
  `vmis[:, :, 1, e]` — the three-index form is a `BoundsError` now, not a silent reinterpretation;
  `matrix_size` is a required keyword of
  `vmi_pipeline`, because the grid is the caller's to state and a `(512, 512, 1)` default silently
  ignored every other one; `rows` without `reduce_rows = true` is refused rather than ignored.
* `vmi_pipeline`'s returned `settings.fbp` is now `settings.recon`: it carries `method`,
  `matrix_size`, `antialias`, `recon_rows`, `filter`, and a `hir` NamedTuple (`strength`,
  `projector`, `reference_kev`, `weights`) when the method is `:hir`, `nothing` otherwise.

#### Added

* **The VMI chain reconstructs its basis pair iteratively as well as analytically** —
  `vmi_pipeline(...; recon_method = :hir, hir_strength = 60, recon_projector, hir_reference_kev = 70)`,
  the same keywords on `reconstruct_basis_slice`. The default stays `:fbp`, the published chain.
  `:hir` changes the reconstructor and nothing else. It is sound on a basis sinogram because of
  three things the analytic arm never needed: each material is weighted by its own inverse
  variance from the K-channel Fisher information (uniformly for `:cong`, which has none), each is
  reconstructed in μ-equivalent units at the reference energy so HIR's Huber threshold and
  strength dial keep the meaning they were tuned for, and a reduced-row sinogram goes through a
  one-row geometry rather than being repeated into rows a one-slice model cannot satisfy. The
  same arm serves a dual-kVp pair decomposed through `spectral_basis_from_acquisitions`.
* **`reconstruct!(::HIRReconWorkspace, ...; weights)`** — `:transmission` (the default, `exp(-y)`
  with the bowtie `air_reference`: right for any log-transmission sinogram, energy-integrating,
  photon-counting summed bins, or either tube voltage of a dual-energy pair), `:uniform`, or an
  explicit per-ray statistical weight map. Until now the transmission weighting was the only one,
  and it was being applied to material-basis sinograms — g/cm², not log-transmissions — in the
  pre-package pipelines, where on the water sinogram (20–30 g/cm² through a body) it clamps to
  e⁻¹⁰ and switches the data term off inside the object while leaving the iodine sinogram
  (≤ 0.2 g/cm²) uniformly weighted. Those "HIR VMIs" were not one reconstructor applied to two
  materials.
* `vmi_pipeline(...; keep_diagnostics = true)` now actually returns the estimator's per-ray maps
  (`diagnostics.fisher`, quality flags); the keyword was accepted and dropped before.
* **`resample_field_to_recon(field, voxel_size, origin, geom, matrix_size; outside)`** — the exact
  box average of a continuous field (a material fraction, a density) onto the reconstruction
  grid, from the axis-aligned overlap of the two grids, as three separable matrix products. This
  is how a fine truth becomes a label volume with the partial volume a reconstruction actually
  sees; `resample_to_recon(…; method = :linear)` point-samples the mask at the voxel centre and
  cannot do it, which the pre-package label notebooks worked around with integer-encoded
  fractional fields and a denominator. `outside` fills the part of an output voxel that lies
  beyond the field's grid (1 for an air fraction), so fractions that sum to one still do.

#### Fixed

* **The wide-beam CTDI rule (N·T > 40 mm) was clamped at the pencil-chamber length and reported
  a 160 mm beam at 100/160 of its value.** 0.15.0's first commit had the IEC Amd 1 expression

  second, independent of the thread count and cached per beam. IEC 60601-2-44 definitions,
  including the reference-beam rule above 40 mm collimation, helical pitch, axial table
  increments, and `overbeam_mm` / `n_tubes` / `dose_calibration` as explicit multipliers.
* **`coverage` for helical reconstructions**: the fraction of half-turn conjugate families that
  found data, per voxel, through `wfbp_helical_reconstruct` and `fdk_reconstruct`. This is how a
  caller tells a reconstructed voxel from an extrapolated one, and it was needed — see below.
* **A per-material path-length cache**: `material_paths(ws, phantom)` walks the volume once and
  `simulate!(...; paths)` reuses it, so a several-kVp study of one phantom pays for the walk
  once. `dd_fast_material_paths!` and `dd_fast_poly_from_paths!` are the projector-level
  entry points.
* `empty_pileup_cache!`, `empty_water_μ_cache!` and `empty_ctdi_cache!` — one per memoised
  table, because a process that has just changed what a cache is keyed on should be able to say
  so. The two array caches hand back a copy, so a caller cannot mutate the stored entry.
* `helical_q` and `mask_fov` reachable through `fdk_reconstruct`, and `simulate!` takes
  `report_dose`, `dose_kwargs`, `paths` and `noise_rng`.

#### Fixed

* **Arc detectors were given the flat panel's row mapping in the helical backprojector.** A
  cylindrical detector has every row at the same in-plane distance from the source, so a voxel's
  height scales by SDD/d; a flat panel sits at perpendicular distance SDD and adds
  1/cos(fan angle). The axial backprojector already branched this way and this one did not.
  Off-centre slice profiles paid for it: thin-disk FWHM at 20 cm radius was 0.95–1.01 mm and
  asymmetric in x, against 0.78–0.81 mm with the mapping the detector actually has.
* **Helical reconstructions extrapolated silently past the data.** At pitch 2 with 32 rows more
  than half the core voxels are not reconstructable and the volume simply read −49 HU mean
  (min −132) in them; about one collimation width at each end of the helix is incomplete at
  every pitch. `coverage` now says so.
* **An odd number of views per rotation biased the whole helical image** by misaligning the
  conjugate families (360 views/rotation −0.01 HU, 361 −2.71, 359 +2.85, 181 +5.69). Warned.
* **Zero-response energies produced NaN in the K-channel estimator.** A real spectrum grid
  reaches down to a few keV where a channel's response is exactly zero and the water term
  overflows Float32; 0·Inf then flagged every ray of a photon-counting scan infeasible and
  skipped the bisection start. Notebook 04 works around this by recomputing the flag on the host
  in Float64.
* `compute_dlp` multiplied the dose-length product by the rotation count a second time.

#### Performance

All bit-identical unless stated.

* Four tube voltages of one phantom: forward projection 4.81 s → 2.16 s on a 1024² × 60 Gammex
  phantom, and 18.06 s → 5.81 s on a 1850 × 1350 × 120 phantom at 0.2 mm, via the path-length
  cache. The cache is sinogram-shaped, so its size does not grow with the phantom grid and the
  saving grows with the resolution.
* The Monte Carlo pile-up migration matrix is memoised: about 17 s, 93 % of building a
  photon-counting workspace, now paid once per process.
* `compute_bowtie_attenuation_spectral` 0.41 s → 0.05 s (no temporaries, thickness interpolated
  per column rather than per column and row).
* The per-column water beam-hardening fit 0.95 s → 0.48 s (the water attenuation table is
  memoised rather than rebuilt per detector column).
* `apply_pcct_noise!(...; rng_mode = :threaded)` draws the photon-counting counts in fixed
  chunks with independent streams: 12.39 s → 3.20 s on 8 threads at 89 M elements, unbiased with
  a Fano factor of 1.000. Opt-in, because it is a different realisation of the same distribution;
  the realisation depends on the seed alone, not on the thread count.

#### Documented

* `src/reconstruction/fbp/wfbp_helical.jl` records what the helical audit measured — uniformity,
  slice sensitivity profile, z-edge error, pitch sweep — and what is not modelled: there is no
  cos(cone angle) weight in the aperture normalisation, which is 0.01–0.10 HU at clinical
  collimations and 2.44 HU at 16 cm.
* `src/source/dose.jl` states what the dose model does not include, in order of size, starting
  with the difference between the IPEM-78 tube output and a particular real tube.
* The API page is on the current API — `EICTScanner` / `PCCTScanner` instead of `Scanner`, the
  `SimOptions` table without the deleted `fidelity` preset and with `projector` and
  `detector_efficiency_mode`, plus new sections for the path cache, dose and CTDI, helical WFBP
  and the K-channel decomposition. The README's three snippets now run as written.

## [0.1.0] - 2026-05-13

### Added
- Initial release, prepping for first General registry submission.
- **Five-struct API**: `Phantom`, `Scanner`, `CTProtocol`, `SimOptions`, `ReconOptions`.
- **Geometry**: cone-beam scanner model with bowtie, focal-spot, and heel-effect modeling. GE Revolution Apex Elite and Siemens NAEOTOM Alpha scanner presets.
- **Forward projection**: exact Siddon ray-voxel intersection (TIGRE-style), GPU-native via AcceleratedKernels.jl (Metal/CUDA/ROCm/CPU).
- **Polychromatic physics**: Beer-Lambert spectral sum with vendor-realistic detector response — MC-derived efficiency LUT for the GE Gemstone Ce:(Tb,Lu)₃Al₅O₁₂ scintillator (captures Tb/Lu K-edge fluorescence escape) and MC-derived response matrix + pile-up matrix for Siemens NAEOTOM Alpha CdTe.
- **Detector physics**: fill factor, scatter (Ohnesorge convolution, geometry- and phantom-size-aware), optical crosstalk, detector lag, focal-spot blur.
- **Reconstruction**: FDK (Feldkamp-Davis-Kress) and Hybrid IR with PWLS refinement.
- **Corrections**: bowtie-aware sinogram + image-domain BHC, low-signal noise floor, radial cupping, PCCT pile-up correction.
- **Spectral imaging**: dual-kVp and PCCT material decomposition (Cong 2022 per-ray Newton, Alvarez-Macovski polynomial calibration, PWLS-L₂ SQS).
- **Virtual monoenergetic imaging**: textbook VMI synthesis + Grant 2014 Mono+ frequency split with phantom-aware masking.
- **Denoising**: sinogram-domain SVD denoiser, image-domain bilateral filter.
- 7 end-to-end Pluto notebooks covering: the Five-Struct API tour, XCAT custom-material phantoms, dual-kVp VMI on Gammex 472, PCCT VMI on Gammex 472, XCAT-to-recon affine grid handling, CatSim vs BasisSim parity benchmark, and a full-resolution true-scan QRM-Thorax reference.
